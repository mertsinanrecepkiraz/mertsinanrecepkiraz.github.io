# Research

<div class="research-grid">

<div class="research-card">
<div class="research-body">
<h2 class="research-title">Machine Learning for Geoscience Data</h2>
<p class="research-desc">Physics-informed and data-driven ML methods for processing large geophysical datasets. I developed trace-by-trace convolutional neural networks that suppress free-surface multiples and ghost reflections, an approach that is robust when acquisition geometries are sparse or irregular. I have also built ML-based QC workflows with automated optimization (Bayesian methods and Optuna), and agentic tools that automate seismic data processing at scale.</p>
</div>
</div>

<div class="research-card">
<div class="research-body">
<h2 class="research-title">Computational Geoscience and High-Performance Computing</h2>
<p class="research-desc">Numerical modeling and imaging of wave propagation, including seismic interferometry, Green's-function retrieval, and full waveform inversion (acoustic, elastic, 3D, and 4D). I write research and production code in Python, C/C++, Fortran, and Julia, using MPI, OpenMP, and GPUs to turn large recorded wavefields into reliable images of complex structure.</p>
</div>
</div>

<div class="research-card">
<div class="research-body">
<h2 class="research-title">Physics-Based Wavefield Methods for Data-Driven Imaging</h2>
<p class="research-desc">Marchenko redatuming and focusing methods that reconstruct wavefields at virtual subsurface locations directly from surface-recorded data, accounting for multiple scattering with minimal model assumptions. My work examines the practical limits of these methods, including sensitivity to background velocity models and the role of wavefield decomposition, so that data-driven imaging can be applied reliably to real datasets.</p>
</div>
</div>

<div class="research-card">
<div class="research-body">
<h2 class="research-title">Subsurface Monitoring for Societal Applications</h2>
<p class="research-desc">Seismic redatuming and imaging to track injected CO2 and other time-lapse changes near a target reservoir, with applications to the Frio carbon sequestration experiment using synthetic and observed vertical seismic profile data. This connects to my work on 4D (time-lapse) imaging and on turning repeated geophysical measurements into actionable information for energy and environmental decisions.</p>
</div>
